We present a modular framework for simulating American-option contract paths while retaining a separately chosen physical stock-return generator. JumpHMM supplies stock paths, an offline neural surface supplies contract variance targets, square-root factors evolve around those targets, and a lattice supplies American-option marks and Greeks. On 234{,}549 observations from 31 tickers and fifteen snapshots, sector-specific neural surfaces reduced in-sample IV root mean squared error from 12.48 to 10.24 volatility percentage points relative to a global parametric shape. Earnings inputs reduced error from 13.03 to 11.98 points on one two-day temporal holdout. A paired ablation compared frozen IV, direct surface evaluation, deterministic mean reversion, uncoupled stochastic factors, and full return-coupled factors on 3{,}000 shared stock paths per ticker for Goldman Sachs and Eli Lilly. After ten trading transitions, the full factors changed interim marks relative to the direct surface by a mean absolute \$1.77--\$2.41 per share across four contracts. Entry marks and terminal payoffs agreed exactly across variants. A strike audit detected residual GS inconsistencies at a \$0.01 tolerance. The results establish sensitivity of interim valuation to the IV module; prospective accuracy and joint no-arbitrage consistency were not established. The implementation and experiment outputs are available for reproduction.
