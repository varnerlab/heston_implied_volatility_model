\subsection{Tests on later dates}\label{sec:chronological_methods}

We tested the sector surfaces on later dates before using them to forecast option prices. The first stage used 58 snapshots labeled April 14 through July 17, 2026, in 53 expanding-window folds. Each fold refitted the networks on earlier snapshots and predicted IV on the next, using that date's observed stock price to compute moneyness. Its root mean squared error (RMSE) therefore measured surface prediction without a stock forecast or an IV update. The second stage fitted six sector networks on 68 sessions through July 31 and fixed them before testing on 23 sessions from August 4 through September 4. We scored IV using observed test-date stock prices, reporting RMSE and bias, the mean predicted minus observed IV. Unlike the first stage, which used files as saved, this stage audited retrieval times, excluded intraday downloads, required two-sided quotes, and computed DTE from the stored close's session date. Training excluded files retrieved after July 31. Retrieval times could not establish quote freshness or exact stock--option synchronization, so timing differences could affect both IV and price errors. Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:chronological_supp} gives the data and fitting rules and the exploratory earnings analysis performed after the July-cutoff surface test. Neither stage tested the per-ticker networks used in the April illustrations.

The option test issued forecasts at successive observed sessions, called origins, from August 4 into early September. At each origin, we selected one put and one call per ticker (GS and LLY) in each of two maturity cohorts using only origin information. Monthly options had 25--45 calendar DTE and were scored one trading session later; shorter-maturity options had 10--21 DTE and were scored one and five trading sessions later. The shorter cohort was added after none of the monthly options remained in the downloaded maturity ladder at five sessions, with this protocol amendment recorded before inspecting aggregate forecast errors. Only exact-contract quotes at the specified endpoint were scored. Forecasts used observed origin stock prices and IVs, with the July-cutoff surfaces, historical JumpHMM parameters, and IV-update constants fixed. All five IV variants shared 1{,}000 stock paths and shocks per origin. Stock states started from the stationary distribution, without conditioning on recent returns, and drift and shock normalization came from an independent pilot. To remove the fitted surface's initial IV-level error from the comparison, we rescaled its variance target at each origin as follows:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:origin_anchor}
\bar v^{*}_{a,t}=\sigma_{a,0,\mathrm{obs}}^2
\frac{\bar v_{a,t}}{\bar v_{a,0}},
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_{a,0,\mathrm{obs}}$ is observed origin IV and $\bar v_{a,t}$ is the surface target in~\eqref{eq:theta}. Frozen IV held the observed value, the direct surface followed $\bar v^{*}_{a,t}$, and the three updating variants reverted toward it with the IV-ablation parameters. This anchoring applied only to forecasts; the ablation retained its fitted starting values.

We fixed the primary score before evaluation: the mean absolute error (MAE) of the forecast mean against the later bid--ask midpoint. We also scored forecast medians and reported signed error, RMSE, and the fraction of forecast means inside the quoted spread. Distributional scores comprised the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), which rewards concentration near the observed value, and central 90\% interval coverage and width. The CRPS definition and simulation settings appear in Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:chronological_supp}. All monetary scores were in dollars per share. Scores weighted origins equally after averaging matched-contract contributions within each ticker, cohort, horizon, and origin. Overlapping horizons and shared market moves made origins dependent; more simulated paths did not increase the market sample size. To assess the contribution of stock-forecast error, we repeated IV updates along observed stock paths when every intervening session was available, retaining the independent IV shocks. A second diagnostic repriced options in the same tree using observed endpoint stock prices and reported IVs to measure the tree's departure from quoted midpoints. Both used future observations and were diagnostics, not forecasts. We also compared JumpHMM stock forecasts with a zero-log-drift Gaussian random walk. Supplementary diagnostics examined simulated IV changes, stock drift and volatility, and earnings-related surface bias.
