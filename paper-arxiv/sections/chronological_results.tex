We extended the surface evaluation beyond the original April--May window and observed uneven transfer to later dates (Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:walk_forward_extended} and~\ref{tab:chronological_surface}). The completed expanding-window experiment evaluated two-input sector networks on 53 successive held-out capture dates through July 17, with a median test RMSE of 9.92 IV percentage points but individual folds ranging from 7.68 to 19.25 points (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:walk_forward_extended}). Each fold used the later observation's stock price to determine moneyness, so the result concerned conditional IV prediction rather than a forecast of the stock--option system. We then fitted a single set of sector surfaces through July 31 and evaluated the 23 later sessions from August 4 through September 4, where pooled RMSE increased from 10.79 points in training to 14.75 points in the held-out sample (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:chronological_surface}). These values were not a paired comparison with the earlier folds because the fitting window, capture audit, and evaluation dates differed. The GS surface had held-out RMSE of 6.30 points and mean overprediction of 0.88 points, whereas LLY had RMSE of 9.96 points and mean overprediction of 4.61 points, more than five times the GS bias. The larger corpus therefore did not remove the problem of a frozen surface level lagging a later market state, which motivated a separate forecast comparison in which all IV variants started from the observed origin IV and differed in their subsequent evolution.

We next compared forward option forecasts with later observations of the same selected contracts and found nearly identical errors under the coupled factor and frozen IV (Table~\ref{tab:forecast_main}). The maturity-bucket collection scheme imposed an immediate limit, because none of the 36 eligible roughly monthly contracts per ticker had an observed exact-symbol endpoint after five sessions (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:forecast_availability}). Their one-session comparison retained 34 quotes across 17 origins per ticker, with coupled-factor MAE of \$3.79 per share for GS and \$6.08 for LLY, compared with \$3.80 and \$6.11 under frozen IV (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:forecast_one_session}). The separately identified shorter-maturity cohort supplied five-session outcomes for 34 GS and 30 LLY quotes across 17 origins per ticker. Coupling again produced only small reductions in the absolute error of the forecast mean, with MAE of \$4.19 versus \$4.21 under frozen IV for GS and \$11.59 versus \$11.62 for LLY, and it improved date-level MAE on six of seventeen GS origins and nine of seventeen LLY origins, so the small average reductions were not consistent across dates. Scoring forecast medians separately lowered GS coupled-factor MAE to \$2.48 and gave LLY MAE of \$11.79, with little separation from frozen IV (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:option_point_summary}). The coupled model's CRPS was 2.01 for GS and 8.93 for LLY, compared with 2.02 and 8.99 under frozen IV. Central 90\% intervals had date-weighted coverage of 100\% for GS and 67.6\% for LLY, with average widths of \$25.65 and \$28.91 per share, so the GS intervals covered every endpoint whereas LLY coverage fell 22 percentage points below the nominal level. In the earliest eligible GS origin, the coupled and frozen-IV mean forecasts nearly coincided while the observed call value declined below both (Fig.~\ref{fig:chronological_forecast}). The fixed stochastic factor therefore showed no material forecasting advantage over frozen IV in this case study.

We then replaced the simulated stock paths with the observed paths and found that stock-forecast error accounted for most of the option-price miss (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:forecast_conditional}). Complete intervening stock observations were available for thirteen five-session origins per ticker, with 26 matched GS quotes and 24 LLY quotes. On these cases, the substitution cut coupled-factor MAE 3.8-fold for GS, from \$4.62 to \$1.23 per share, and 5.4-fold for LLY, from \$10.61 to \$1.95. The observed path was unknown at the origin, so this diagnostic located the error rather than correcting it. Within the diagnostic, frozen IV had lower point errors of \$1.07 and \$1.74, while uncoupled stochastic factors had lower CRPS than the coupled factor for both tickers. The retained JumpHMM settings also gave no point-forecast advantage for the stock itself, with five-session stock MAE of \$16.85 for GS and \$49.83 for LLY versus \$16.17 and \$48.64 for the simple random walk (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:forecast_stock}). Repricing the shorter-maturity endpoint observations with their reported IV reduced five-session option MAE to \$0.14 for GS and \$0.34 for LLY, and those prices lay within every observed bid--ask interval (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:forecast_repricing}). Sampled lattice refinement changed marks by at most \$0.0026 per share across the two cohorts, below the forecast errors. Stock-forecast error therefore dominated the option-price misses, smaller errors remained in the IV and pricing inputs, and no dynamic IV variant was reliably superior.
