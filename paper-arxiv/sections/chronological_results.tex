Tests on later dates identified shifts in IV bias, and an exploratory refit with earnings inputs reduced LLY's bias after its August report. Across 53 next-snapshot folds, median RMSE was 9.92 volatility points, with a range of 7.68--19.25 (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:walk_forward_extended}). The separate July-cutoff fit rose from 10.79 points in training to 14.75 on the 23 August--September sessions and overpredicted IV by 6.88 points on average (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:chronological_surface}). Its data screening and test period differed from those of the folds, so the two errors were not directly comparable. The held-out bias was 0.88 points for GS and 4.61 for LLY. Around LLY's August 5 earnings report, the surface changed from underpredicting IV by 17.90 points on August 4 to overpredicting it on every observed date from August 6 onward (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:mechanism_checks}). In a subsequent exploratory refit, earnings inputs reduced LLY's average bias over August 6--September 4 from 6.17 to 3.85 points. The 38\% reduction fell short of the protocol's halving criterion, and substantial bias remained (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:earnings_refit}). To keep these initial level errors out of the option comparison, every forecast variant started from observed IV and used a surface target rescaled to match it.

In the five-session GS and LLY tests, the full IV update produced mean option-price forecasts with errors close to those under fixed IV (Table~\ref{tab:forecast_main}). The five-session comparison used the shorter-maturity cohort: the monthly cohort had no exact-contract outcomes at that horizon (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:forecast_availability}). Seventeen origins per ticker supplied 34 GS and 30 LLY quotes. Compared with frozen IV, the full factor reduced mean-forecast MAE only from \$4.21 to \$4.19 per share for GS and from \$11.62 to \$11.59 for LLY. It improved on only six and nine of the seventeen origins, respectively. Median-forecast errors also showed no advantage, and one-session monthly forecasts gave similarly small differences (Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:option_point_summary} and~\ref{tab:forecast_one_session}). The full factor's distribution score, CRPS, was close to frozen IV's, while its central 90\% intervals covered all GS outcomes but only 67.6\% of LLY outcomes. The example forecast likewise showed nearly coincident full-factor and frozen-IV means (Fig.~\ref{fig:chronological_forecast}). Over five sessions, average full-factor IV moved only 0.30 volatility points for GS and 0.18 for LLY, although individual paths moved by about 2.5 points in absolute terms (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:mechanism_checks}). The slow update closed only about a quarter of the implied-variance gap to a fixed target, and shocks partly canceled across paths. These small mean IV changes were consistent with the similar option forecasts despite larger changes on individual paths.

Using observed stock paths greatly reduced option errors, showing the importance of stock forecasting to the joint forecast (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:forecast_conditional}). On the thirteen origins per ticker with complete stock histories, the full-factor MAE fell from \$4.62 to \$1.23 for GS and from \$10.61 to \$1.95 for LLY. These were paired comparisons on the same 26 GS and 24 LLY quotes; the observed paths were future information and served only as a diagnostic. Repricing with both endpoint stock prices and reported IVs gave much smaller MAEs, \$0.14 and \$0.34, although that check used all seventeen matched origins rather than thirteen (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:forecast_repricing}). Tree refinement changed sampled prices by at most \$0.0026 (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:forecast_numerical_checks}). Together, these checks pointed to stock and IV prediction rather than tree accuracy as the main sources of error. Even with stock paths known, frozen IV had lower MAE than the full factor: \$1.07 versus \$1.23 for GS and \$1.74 versus \$1.95 for LLY. Random IV shocks did improve conditional distribution scores relative to frozen IV, but the uncoupled factor's CRPS of \$0.71 and \$1.35 was lower than the full factor's \$0.90 and \$1.53 (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:forecast_conditional}). Coupling therefore did not improve these conditional distributions, and no IV update consistently improved point forecasts.
