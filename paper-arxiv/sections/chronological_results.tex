We extended the surface evaluation beyond the original April--May window and observed uneven accuracy on later dates (Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:walk_forward_extended} and~\ref{tab:chronological_surface}). In the expanding-window test, two-input sector networks predicted 53 successive held-out capture dates through July 17. The median test RMSE was 9.92 IV percentage points, but individual folds ranged from 7.68 to 19.25 points (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:walk_forward_extended}). We then fitted one set of sector surfaces through July 31 and scored them on the 23 later sessions from August 4 through September 4. Pooled RMSE rose from 10.79 points in training to 14.75 points on the held-out sessions (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:chronological_surface}). The fitting window, capture audit, and evaluation dates differed from those of the earlier folds, so the two sets of errors cannot be compared directly. The GS surface had held-out RMSE of 6.30 points and overpredicted IV by 0.88 points on average. LLY had RMSE of 9.96 points and was overpredicted by 4.61 points, more than five times the GS bias (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:chronological_surface}). Even with the larger data set, a fixed surface level still lagged later market IV. This lag motivated a separate forecast comparison in which all IV variants started from the observed IV at the origin and differed only in how they evolved afterward.

We next compared option forecasts with later observations of the same options and found nearly identical errors under the coupled factor and frozen IV (Table~\ref{tab:forecast_main}). None of the 36 eligible roughly one-month options per ticker was observed again five sessions later, so these options were scored after one session only (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:forecast_availability}). That comparison kept 34 quotes across 17 origins per ticker. Coupled-factor MAE was \$3.79 per share for GS and \$6.08 for LLY, compared with \$3.80 and \$6.11 under frozen IV (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:forecast_one_session}). The shorter-maturity cohort supplied five-session outcomes for 34 GS and 30 LLY quotes across 17 origins per ticker. Coupling again reduced the MAE of the forecast mean only slightly, to \$4.19 from \$4.21 under frozen IV for GS and to \$11.59 from \$11.62 for LLY. It improved date-level MAE on six of seventeen GS origins and nine of seventeen LLY origins, so these small average reductions were not consistent across dates (Table~\ref{tab:forecast_main}). Scoring the forecast median instead lowered GS coupled-factor MAE to \$2.48 and gave LLY an MAE of \$11.79, again close to frozen IV (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:option_point_summary}). The coupled factor's CRPS was also close to that of frozen IV, at 2.01 versus 2.02 for GS and 8.93 versus 8.99 for LLY (Table~\ref{tab:forecast_main}). Date-weighted coverage of the central 90\% intervals was 100\% for GS but 67.6\% for LLY, 22 percentage points below the nominal level. At the earliest eligible GS origin, the coupled and frozen-IV mean forecasts nearly coincided, while the observed call value fell below both (Fig.~\ref{fig:chronological_forecast}).

We then replaced the simulated stock paths with the observed paths and found that stock-forecast error accounted for most of the option-price error (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:forecast_conditional}). Every intervening stock price was observed for thirteen five-session origins per ticker, which gave 26 matched GS quotes and 24 LLY quotes. On these cases, the substitution cut coupled-factor MAE 3.8-fold for GS, from \$4.62 to \$1.23 per share, and 5.4-fold for LLY, from \$10.61 to \$1.95 (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:forecast_conditional}). Because the observed path was unknown at the origin, this diagnostic located the source of the error. Within the diagnostic, frozen IV had lower point errors, \$1.07 for GS and \$1.74 for LLY, and the uncoupled stochastic factor had lower CRPS than the coupled factor for both tickers. JumpHMM also gave no point-forecast advantage for the stock itself. Its five-session stock MAE was \$16.85 for GS and \$49.83 for LLY, compared with \$16.17 and \$48.64 for the simple random walk (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:forecast_stock}). Repricing each shorter-maturity option at its endpoint with its reported IV reduced five-session option MAE to \$0.14 for GS and \$0.34 for LLY. Every repriced value lay within its observed bid--ask interval (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:forecast_repricing}). Refining the tree on sampled paths changed prices by at most \$0.0026 per share across the two cohorts, below the forecast errors (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:forecast_numerical_checks}). Stock-forecast error therefore dominated the option-price misses, smaller errors remained in the IV and pricing inputs, and no IV variant was reliably better than the others.
