Recomputing prices from the saved stock and variance paths reproduced the forecast means, quantiles, and CRPS. Increasing the tree from 201 to 401 steps changed sampled prices by at most \$0.0026 per share (Table~\ref{tab:forecast_numerical_checks}). At the first origin for each ticker, the paired Monte Carlo standard errors of coupled-minus-frozen mean prices ranged from \$0.041 to \$0.061 across the four shorter-maturity options at five sessions (Table~\ref{tab:forecast_numerical_checks}). These standard errors measure simulation precision for a single price difference, not variation across market dates. The small average MAE reductions therefore do not show that the coupled factor forecasts better (Table~\ref{tab:forecast_main}). In the observed-stock diagnostic, frozen IV, the direct surface, and deterministic mean reversion gave a single price once the stock path was fixed, so their central intervals had zero width. The stochastic factors' intervals reflected IV uncertainty alone, so their coverage measures something different from the coverage of the joint forecasts (Table~\ref{tab:forecast_conditional}). The saved score files include both cohorts, every horizon with outcomes, all five IV variants, and the date-level errors.

Several quantities explain the forecast outcomes (Table~\ref{tab:mechanism_checks}). With $\kappa=15$ and $\Delta t=1/252$, the Euler update in~\eqref{eq:euler} retained a fraction $1-\kappa\Delta t\approx0.94$ of a factor's deterministic gap to a fixed target at each session. This gave a half-life of 11.3 sessions and closed 26.4\% of the gap after five sessions and 45.9\% after ten. The five-session rows used the seventeen scored origins per ticker and the matched contracts in Table~\ref{tab:forecast_main}. Their IV changes compared simulated IV five sessions after origin with the observed origin IV, averaging over paths and then contracts. The one-session volatility rows used the stock-benchmark origins in Table~\ref{tab:stock_validation}. Realized values were sample standard deviations of the observed one-session log returns, and EWMA values averaged the adaptive-volatility model's origin estimates. The GS EWMA estimate began from its 2014--2024 sample variance and rose through the April--July 2026 returns before the first August origin. The surface-bias rows break the held-out ticker bias in Table~\ref{tab:chronological_surface} down by underlying session. The report rows average each ticker's daily bias over the five trading days before and after its latest report, using training and held-out days alike. The public earnings calendar dated LLY's report on August 5, 2026, after the training cutoff, and GS's on July 14, 2026, inside the training window.

We tested the earnings explanation by refitting the July surfaces with the two earnings inputs used in the April holdout (Table~\ref{tab:earnings_refit}). A protocol written before fitting kept the two-input surfaces' training and test rows, architecture, and training schedule. It fitted seeds 42, 43, and 44 for both versions, with the underlying session as the observation date. The protocol called the explanation supported if the earnings inputs at least halved LLY's mean absolute bias from August 6 through September 4, with every earnings-aware seed below every two-input seed. It called the explanation not supported if they did not reduce the bias. The earnings inputs reduced that bias from 6.17 to 3.85 volatility points on average, and every earnings-aware seed fell below every two-input seed. The 38\% reduction fell short of half, so the prespecified verdict was partial. The inputs also reduced LLY's underprediction on August 4 from 17.90 to 12.86 points and pooled held-out RMSE from 14.75 to 13.94 points, while GS's held-out RMSE changed by less than 0.1 point. The two-input refits varied by less than 0.05 points across seeds in every reported quantity, so these differences exceed seed variation. Each sector network shares one earnings response across its tickers, so the refit could represent a typical report effect but not one sized to each ticker. LLY's bias shifted about five times as much as GS's across their latest reports (Table~\ref{tab:mechanism_checks}).
