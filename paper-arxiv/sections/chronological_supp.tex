\section{Details of the Tests on Later Dates}\label{sec:chronological_supp}

The surface tests used different data and fitting rules. The first refitted the two-input sector networks from scratch on the first $k$ of 58 snapshots and tested the next, for $k=5,\ldots,57$, using the calibration architecture and training schedule. Each fold standardized inputs from its training rows alone. An independent reconstruction reproduced the expanding-window dataset's 986{,}492 filtered observations and confirmed that all passed the July-cutoff fit's two-sided-quote checks. For the July-cutoff fit, we accepted files retrieved between 20:00 UTC on the stored close's session date and 13:30 UTC on the next trading day, the New York close-to-open interval during April--September. We retained the latest eligible file per ticker and session, removed duplicate option rows, and excluded twenty intraday files. Quotes required finite positive bids, asks no lower than bids, and positive displayed sizes. Surface fitting retained the calibration IV and moneyness filters and required positive DTE recomputed from expiration and session date. Training also excluded retrievals after July 31, 2026, UTC. The 68 training sessions ran from April 13 through July 31 and supplied 1{,}173{,}538 observations. The six networks used seed 42 and the calibration training schedule of at most 2{,}000 epochs. We retained their final weights and ticker levels and used training-only standardization for both fitting and testing. Testing used 425{,}061 observations from 23 sessions between August 4 and September 4; August 3 and 20 had no snapshots. Tables~\ref{tab:walk_forward_extended} and~\ref{tab:chronological_surface} report the fold and July-cutoff errors separately, and Table~\ref{tab:chronological_data_audit} records the data checks.

The forecast protocol fixed contract selection, simulation, and scoring before aggregate errors were inspected; its maturity amendment responded to missing outcomes. Within the monthly and shorter-maturity ranges, we chose expirations nearest 31 and 14 calendar DTE, respectively, then strikes nearest $K/S=0.95$ for puts and $1.05$ for calls. Ties favored earlier expirations and lower strikes. Missing exact-contract endpoints remained missing, without replacing contracts or dates. Monthly contracts moved between the collector's 14- and 30-day targets after five sessions and were no longer saved; shorter contracts moved from the 14-day target toward the 7-day target and usually remained available (Table~\ref{tab:forecast_availability}). The observed-stock diagnostic required every intervening close and therefore used a smaller subset shared by all variants. Simulations used fixed ticker-and-origin seeds and the 2014--2024 JumpHMM fit, starting from a stationary state with no jump episode in progress. A separate 10{,}000-path pilot per ticker, reused at every origin, fixed the shift to the assumed 10\% annualized log-growth target and the stock-return mean and standard deviation. The random walk used zero log drift and the pilot return standard deviation. The observed-stock diagnostic standardized realized returns with the same pilot constants, and endpoint repricing used the 201-step LR tree. We selected the earliest GS shorter-cohort origin with both five-session outcomes for the example figure, independently of forecast errors. For endpoint values $P_1,\ldots,P_N$ on $N=N_p$ paths and observed midpoint $y$, the empirical continuous ranked probability score~\cite{gneiting2007} was given by:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forecast_crps}
\mathrm{CRPS}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|P_j-y|
-\frac{1}{2N^2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{l=1}^{N}|P_j-P_l|,
\end{equation}
Smaller CRPS indicated better forecasts. Coverage was the fraction of observed midpoints inside the central 90\% interval of simulated values; interval width measured its spread. For each metric, we averaged its matched-contract contributions within each ticker, cohort, horizon, and origin, then averaged origins. For RMSE, we took the square root of the resulting mean squared error. This gave dates equal weight.

Before interpreting small differences between IV variants, we checked that the forecast prices were reproducible and numerically accurate. Recomputing prices from the saved stock and variance paths reproduced the forecast means, quantiles, and CRPS. Increasing the tree from 201 to 401 steps changed sampled prices by at most \$0.0026 per share (Table~\ref{tab:forecast_numerical_checks}). At the first origin for each ticker, the paired Monte Carlo standard errors of the mean price difference between the full factor and frozen IV ranged from \$0.041 to \$0.061 across the four shorter-maturity options at five sessions (Table~\ref{tab:forecast_numerical_checks}). These standard errors measure simulation precision for a single price difference, not variation across market dates. These checks do not establish a forecasting advantage from the small average MAE reductions (Table~\ref{tab:forecast_main}). In the observed-stock diagnostic, frozen IV, the direct surface, and mean reversion each gave a single price once the stock path was fixed, so their central intervals had zero width. The stochastic factors' intervals reflected IV uncertainty alone, so their coverage measures something different from the coverage of the joint forecasts (Table~\ref{tab:forecast_conditional}). The saved score files include both cohorts, every horizon with outcomes, all five IV variants, and the date-level errors.

Four diagnostics help interpret the forecasts: IV-factor persistence, median stock-price change, stock volatility, and surface bias (Table~\ref{tab:mechanism_checks}). With $\kappa=15$ and $\Delta t=1/252$, the Euler update in~\eqref{eq:euler} retained a fraction $1-\kappa\Delta t\approx0.94$ of a factor's deterministic gap to a fixed target at each session. This retention gave a half-life of 11.3 sessions, so the update closed 26.4\% of the gap after five sessions and 45.9\% after ten. The five-session rows used the seventeen scored origins per ticker and the matched contracts in Table~\ref{tab:forecast_main}. Their IV changes compared simulated IV five sessions after origin with the observed origin IV, averaging over paths and then contracts. The one-session volatility rows used the stock-benchmark origins in Table~\ref{tab:stock_validation}. Realized values were sample standard deviations of the observed one-session log returns, and EWMA values averaged the adaptive-volatility model's origin estimates. The GS EWMA estimate began from its 2014--2024 sample variance and rose through the April--July 2026 returns before the first August origin. The surface-bias rows break the held-out ticker bias in Table~\ref{tab:chronological_surface} down by underlying session. The report rows average each ticker's daily bias over the five observed sessions before and after its latest report. They exclude the report day, weight each session by its number of observations, and use training and held-out days alike. The public earnings calendar dated LLY's report on August 5, 2026, after the training cutoff, and GS's on July 14, 2026, inside the training window.

The timing of LLY's report, after the training cutoff, suggested an earnings explanation for its held-out bias. We tested this explanation by refitting the July surfaces with the two earnings inputs used in the April holdout (Table~\ref{tab:earnings_refit}). A protocol written before refitting retained the training and test rows, hidden-layer sizes, and training schedule. Both versions used seeds 42, 43, and 44 and underlying-session dates. For each seed, we averaged LLY's signed IV errors over all observations from August 6 through September 4, then took the absolute value. Support required the average of these absolute biases across seeds to fall by at least half, with every earnings-aware seed below every two-input seed. No reduction meant no support; intermediate outcomes meant partial support. The earnings inputs reduced that bias from 6.17 to 3.85 volatility points on average, and every earnings-aware seed fell below every two-input seed. The 38\% reduction fell short of half, so the prespecified verdict was partial. The inputs also reduced LLY's underprediction on August 4 from 17.90 to 12.86 points and held-out RMSE pooled across all tickers from 14.75 to 13.94 points. The held-out RMSE for GS changed by less than 0.1 point. The two-input refits varied by less than 0.05 points across seeds in every reported quantity. Each sector network shared its earnings-response shape, with ticker-specific levels but no ticker-specific earnings parameters.
