\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

In this study, we built a simulator that values American options along stock paths from a separately chosen stock model, here the jump hidden Markov model JumpHMM. It updated each option's IV on every simulated date. Each option's implied variance moved partway each day toward the prediction of a neural surface fitted to past quotes. This update followed the mean-reverting variance equation of the Heston model with random shocks. A binomial tree then converted the resulting IV into the option's price. More flexible surfaces, fitted separately to smaller groups of tickers, matched the fitting data more closely, but their accuracy on later dates was uneven. Repricing identical stock paths under different IV updates changed option values before expiration and the outcome of closing positions early, while payoffs at expiration stayed the same. In forecasts of later option prices, the IV update with random shocks linked to stock returns showed no consistent advantage over holding IV fixed. Repeating the forecasts with the stock paths that actually occurred reduced option errors, which pointed to the stock forecast as the main source of error. In stock forecasts for 2025 and for August and September 2026, JumpHMM, an adaptive-volatility model, and a direction-predicting model all predicted prices about as accurately as assuming no change. The adaptive-volatility model improved the predicted range of stock prices for LLY in both periods but not for GS. Tuning on 2019--2024 data found no benefit from predicting direction with recent stock and market returns. The direction-predicting model therefore gave the same forecasts as the adaptive-volatility model. The simulator therefore supports reproducible comparisons of IV and stock assumptions, but it did not forecast better than simpler alternatives. Saved observations, fitted models, simulation outputs, and scoring code make every comparison reviewable. With complete option histories and prices made consistent across strikes, the same approach could supply realistic option prices for testing trading and hedging systems on market paths that never happened.
