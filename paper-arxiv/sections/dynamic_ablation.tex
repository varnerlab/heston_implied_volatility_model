Repricing the same four options on identical stock paths isolated the effect of the IV rule before expiration (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:dynamic_ablation}). After ten trading days, across 3{,}000 paths, the full factor's mean absolute price difference from the direct surface was \$1.77--\$2.41 per share, compared with \$0.51--\$2.23 for frozen IV and \$0.42--\$1.82 for mean reversion. Similar differences across three seeds showed that lag alone changed prices, while random shocks added larger pathwise changes (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:ablation_seeds}). Mean P\&L changed less because positive and negative differences partly canceled. Linking IV shocks to stock returns, or coupling, then shifted short-position P\&L from falling-stock paths toward rising-stock paths (Fig.~\ref{fig:dynamic_ablation}). Relative to the uncoupled factor, grouped path comparisons showed deeper put losses when stocks fell and smaller call losses when stocks rose. For GS, coupling changed the 5\% expected shortfall, the mean P\&L in the worst 5\% of outcomes, from $-\$62.98$ to $-\$64.60$ for the put and from $-\$71.53$ to $-\$69.82$ for the call. The LLY shifts had the same signs, and all four were at least seven times their paired-bootstrap simulation standard errors (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:dynamic_ablation}). Entry prices and terminal payoffs were identical across variants, so an expiration-only comparison would have missed these effects.

The differences between IV variants were much larger than the price changes under tree refinement. Increasing the tree from 201 to 401 steps changed sampled prices by at most \$0.0017 per share; no factor reached its variance floor, and only 0.7--2.9\% of simulated dates for the four illustrated options left the fitted moneyness range (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:ablation_seeds}). In the strike audit, the GS direct surface failed 178 of 1{,}818 convexity checks by more than \$0.01, with a largest excess of \$0.113 per share. These failures implied negative-priced butterfly spreads. It also failed 68 monotonicity and 68 vertical-spread checks, on different paths and dates (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:strike_audit}). Violations occurred after entry as stock prices and maturities changed, including where audit strikes required extrapolation. The updating variants had no convexity violations and at most three monotonicity violations, the largest \$0.014; frozen IV and all LLY variants passed every check at the \$0.01 threshold. Counts were unchanged at 201 and 401 steps (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:strike_audit_depth}). The failures therefore persisted under tree refinement; passing these limited checks did not establish an arbitrage-free model.
