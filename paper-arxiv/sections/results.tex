\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

More flexible IV surfaces lowered pooled fitting error, with the per-ticker models giving the closest pooled fit (Table~\ref{tab:three_way}; Figs.~\ref{fig:per_ticker_panels} and~\ref{fig:price_error}). Across 234{,}549 observations from 31 tickers, root mean squared error (RMSE), measured in volatility percentage points, fell from 12.48 for the global parametric surface to 11.47 for a global neural surface and 10.24 for sector networks. Per-ticker networks, with sector fallbacks for PFE and BMY, reduced it to 9.73 points, 22\% below the parametric fit. Of the 29 tickers with their own network, 21 improved on their sector fit; the eight exceptions used smaller networks (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:per_ticker_comparison}). These pooled gains did not remove errors within the calibration period. On May 11, the per-ticker fits overpredicted near-the-money IV by 9.45 points for MSFT and 7.90 for LLY, while underpredicting AVGO by 5.37 points (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:smile_date_bias}). Each surface had one ticker level across all fifteen snapshots and could not shift it for a particular date. Pricing the six illustrated tickers with their per-ticker fits showed why dollar errors also mattered. Only 20\% of MSFT absolute price errors were within the contract's bid--ask half-spread, compared with 88\% for GS (Fig.~\ref{fig:price_error}; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:price_error_summary}). These shares also depended on spread width. For example, only about one third of SPY prices fell inside their spreads despite small IV bias; its median half-spread was \$0.03, compared with \$1.56 for GS. For LLY, replacing fitted IV with reported IV in the same tree cut median absolute price error from \$4.01 to \$0.67 per share. Thus, even an in-sample surface error could materially affect option prices.

Adding earnings-date inputs reduced sector-surface prediction errors in the April holdout (Table~\ref{tab:earnings_holdout}). With May 11 excluded from the sector fits, pooled RMSE was 10.21 points in training and 10.89 on that date, with the largest increases in energy and retail (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:loo_sector_nn}). The April earnings holdout produced a larger gap: configuration A, using moneyness and maturity alone, rose from 8.05 to 13.03 points. Removing observations near earnings in configuration B removed the gap, but changed both samples and discarded most rows, so it did not establish that earnings caused the gap in A. Configuration C used the same rows as A and added earnings-date inputs, reducing test RMSE to 11.98 points. Improvements varied across technology tickers (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:tech_pivot}). Correcting the one-day DTE offset in the April 23 snapshot raised test RMSE by 0.14--0.19 points without changing these comparisons (Table~\ref{tab:earnings_holdout}). Earnings inputs therefore reduced the transfer error only partly. These holdouts tested sector networks, not the per-ticker surfaces used in the GS and LLY illustrations.
