We then turned to the simulator, with the fitted surfaces as targets, and observed that simulated GS option values tracked the stock, while the put and call IV followed different paths (Figs.~\ref{fig:gs_paths} and~\ref{fig:gs_iv}). We selected one put and one call for each of GS and LLY from the April 28 capture, all expiring on May 29 (Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:gs_scenario} and~\ref{tab:lly_scenario}). The original illustrations used 1{,}000 stock paths per ticker and a separate IV factor for each option, with the fixed settings in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:scenario_settings}. Before expiration, the put and call factors followed different targets as moneyness and calendar DTE changed (Fig.~\ref{fig:gs_iv}). The LLY paths used the same calculation with LLY's own fitted return distribution and a higher fitted IV level (Supplementary Figs.~\ref{fig:lly_paths} and~\ref{fig:lly_iv}). For both tickers, the Greeks along each path showed delta approaching $\pm1$ on paths heading toward exercise, gamma concentrating near the strike, and Vega declining toward expiration (Supplementary Figs.~\ref{fig:gs_greeks} and~\ref{fig:lly_greeks}). Because the surfaces included observations from the scenario period, these paths illustrated how the model behaves. The tests on later dates evaluated forecasts with separately fitted surfaces.

We next held each short position to expiration and observed that the seller usually kept the full premium but faced losses in the lower tail (Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:gs_scenario} and~\ref{tab:lly_scenario}). For GS, the short put kept its full premium on 74.0\% of paths and the short call on 67.7\%. The worst 5\% of outcomes, however, lost at least \$58.96 per share for the put and \$86.18 for the call. These tail losses were 3.6 and 5.4 times the entry premiums, and they left mean P\&L positive for the put but negative for the call. The market heuristic $1-|\Delta|$, which estimates the chance of keeping the premium from the quoted delta, gave 70.5\% and 67.2\% for the same contracts. For LLY, the put and call kept their premiums on 80.7\% and 79.3\% of paths, and both had positive mean P\&L. These frequencies exceeded the heuristic by 11.0 and 10.2 percentage points, larger gaps than in GS. Agreement between the heuristic and the simulated frequencies therefore depended on the ticker and scenario. Quote delta comes from a risk-neutral pricing calculation, whereas the simulated frequencies came from real-world stock paths. Because P\&L at expiration depended only on the stock path, strike, and premium, these results reflected the return model and the contracts, not the IV updates. Testing the IV updates required changing them while holding the stock paths fixed.
