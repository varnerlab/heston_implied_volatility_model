\subsection{Stock forecast benchmarks}\label{sec:stock_validation_methods}

The option forecast errors led us to evaluate the stock forecasts separately. We first checked how returns were scaled and how prices were rebuilt from them. We then repeated the 2026 stock forecasts with the starting hidden state inferred from recent returns. This diagnostic kept the fitted parameters and, during state inference, tracked the remaining length of any jump episode in progress. We then compared four stock forecast methods: the saved 2014--2024 JumpHMM, an unchanged-price benchmark, a Gaussian model with adaptive volatility, and the same volatility model with a simple directional regression. JumpHMM kept its long-run starting state and pilot growth shift. The adaptive model updated its daily variance with an exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) of squared log returns. The directional regression estimated a daily drift from the stock's cumulative returns over its latest one, five, and twenty valid days and SPY's cumulative return over its latest five valid days. Each feature was divided by the origin volatility and the square root of its window length. A ridge penalty with no intercept shrank the coefficients toward zero. The adaptive and directional models were given by:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stock_benchmark}
a_t=\lambda a_{t-1}+(1-\lambda)u_t^2,
\qquad
\log\frac{S_{t+h}}{S_t}=h\mu_t+\sqrt{a_t}\sum_{j=1}^{h}Z_j,
\quad Z_j\sim\mathcal N(0,1),
\end{equation}
where $u_t=\log(S_t/S_{t-1})$ is the observed daily log return, $a_t$ is the estimated daily variance at origin $t$, $\lambda$ is the EWMA decay, $h$ is the horizon in trading days, and $\mu_t$ is the daily drift. The adaptive model set $\mu_t=0$, and the directional model used the regression estimate. Drift and variance stayed fixed within each forecast horizon, so neither model used future stock or market returns. The unchanged-price benchmark gave only a point forecast, with no uncertainty interval. These stock tests left the paths used in the option forecasts and the IV ablation unchanged.

We chose the new models' settings on annual validation blocks from 2019 through 2024. Each block's regression fit and feature scaling used only earlier data. We chose one EWMA decay and one ridge penalty for both tickers, weighting each ticker, validation year, and horizon equally. Candidate decays were $0.80$, $0.90$, $0.94$, $0.97$, and $0.99$, and ridge penalties ranged from $0.01$ to $1000$ and included the zero-drift limit. Decay selection minimized Gaussian log loss for cumulative log returns, while penalty selection minimized squared cumulative-return error divided by forecast variance. After selection, we fitted the regression on 2014--2024 observations and froze its coefficients and scaling. The 2025 test supplied 249 one-session and 245 five-session origins per ticker. The August--September 2026 test kept the original 21 one-session and 17 five-session stock outcomes per ticker. All methods used identical starting prices and endpoints. Features and variance at each origin used only data available at that time. Missing closes were left as gaps, so every return used spanned one trading day. Each stochastic model used 10{,}000 paths per origin and three fixed seeds, and the adaptive and directional models shared the same Gaussian shocks. We reported the MAE of the median forecast and the RMSE of the mean forecast, because the median minimizes expected absolute error and the mean minimizes expected squared error~\cite{hyndman2021}. We also reported CRPS and the coverage and width of central 90\% intervals. Scores were averaged across dates within each simulation seed and then across seeds. We proposed these model classes after seeing the original 2026 misses, although their settings came from the 2019--2024 validation blocks. We therefore reported the two periods separately and treated the 2026 follow-up as exploratory. Every JumpHMM simulation used the truncated emissions in~\eqref{eq:truncated_emission}, which also supplied the likelihoods for inferring the starting state. The Gaussian benchmarks and their settings did not depend on the truncation.
