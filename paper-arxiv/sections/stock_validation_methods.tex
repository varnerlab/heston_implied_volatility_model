\subsection{Stock forecast benchmarks}\label{sec:stock_validation_methods}

We investigated JumpHMM's stock-forecast errors with separate 10{,}000-path runs, leaving the option test's 1{,}000-path forecasts and the IV-ablation paths unchanged. We checked the conversion of annualized growth rates to daily log returns with the $1/252$ step and daily compounding by reconstructing prices from saved growth rates. An initialization diagnostic repeated the 2026 forecasts with states inferred from available past returns. Its forward filter kept the fitted parameters and tracked both the hidden state and remaining jump duration using the truncated-emission likelihoods in~\eqref{eq:truncated_emission}. The four-method benchmark instead retained JumpHMM's stationary initialization and pilot growth shift, comparing it with unchanged-price, adaptive-volatility, and direction-predicting forecasts. The forecast test's random walk and filtered initialization remained separate diagnostics. The unchanged-price benchmark predicted the origin price without an uncertainty interval. The adaptive-volatility model used zero-drift Gaussian log returns with variance estimated by an exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA)~\cite{riskmetrics1996}; the direction-predicting model added a regression-based drift. Their variance update and forecast return were given by:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stock_benchmark}
a_t=\lambda a_{t-1}+(1-\lambda)u_t^2,
\qquad
\log\frac{S_{t+h}}{S_t}=h\mu_t+\sqrt{a_t}\sum_{j=1}^{h}Z_j,
\quad Z_j\sim\mathcal N(0,1),
\end{equation}
where $u_t=\log(S_t/S_{t-1})$ is the observed daily log return, $a_t$ is daily variance estimated at origin $t$, $\lambda$ is its decay, and $h$ is the horizon in trading days. The adaptive-volatility model set daily drift $\mu_t=0$; the direction-predicting model used the regression below. Both held drift and variance fixed within the horizon. Only JumpHMM used the return cutoff.

The direction regression predicted the next daily log return divided by origin EWMA volatility; multiplying its prediction by that volatility gave $\mu_t$. Its four inputs were the stock's cumulative log returns over its latest one, five, and twenty daily returns, and SPY's cumulative log return over its latest five daily returns. The fund SPY tracks the S\&P 500. Each was divided by the square root of its window length and its own asset's origin EWMA volatility, then by that input's training root mean square. We fitted GS and LLY separately without intercepts, using ridge penalties to shrink coefficients toward zero~\cite{hoerl1970}. Annual validation blocks from 2019 through 2024 used only earlier data to fit coefficients and input scales. We selected one decay and one penalty for both tickers, weighting ticker, validation year, and horizon (one and five trading sessions) equally. Candidate decays were $0.80$, $0.90$, $0.94$, $0.97$, and $0.99$; selection minimized Gaussian log loss of cumulative log returns under zero drift. Candidate penalties ranged from $0.01$ to $1000$, plus the zero-drift limit of an infinite penalty, which sets all coefficients to zero. With the selected decay fixed, penalty selection minimized squared cumulative-return error divided by forecast variance. We then fitted coefficients and input scales on 2014--2024 observations and froze them.

We tested all four methods on 2025 and August--September 2026 data, using identical origin prices and endpoints. Historical closes came from the code's S\&P 500 daily price files; 2026 closes came from the option files. The latter began in April, when the EWMA variance restarted from its 2014--2024 estimate. The 2025 test had 249 one-session and 245 five-session origins per ticker; the 2026 test used option-test origins with observed endpoints, giving 21 and 17, respectively. Origin features and variance used only available past data. We computed daily returns only from closes on adjacent trading sessions, omitting returns across gaps. Each stochastic method used 10{,}000 paths per origin under three fixed seeds, with Gaussian shocks shared by the adaptive-volatility and direction-predicting models. We scored median forecasts by MAE and mean forecasts by RMSE, matching each summary to the loss it minimizes~\cite{hyndman2021}; the option test retained its prespecified mean-forecast MAE. For the three stochastic methods, distributional scores were CRPS and central 90\% interval coverage and width. Scores averaged dates within each seed, then seeds. To interpret interval widths, we compared JumpHMM's pilot daily log-return standard deviation with the observed test-period value and the adaptive-volatility estimate averaged over origins (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:mechanism_checks}). Although settings came from 2019--2024 validation, we proposed the alternative models after seeing the 2026 errors. We therefore reported the periods separately and treated the 2026 comparison as exploratory.
