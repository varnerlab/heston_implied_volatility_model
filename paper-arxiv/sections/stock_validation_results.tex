Adaptive volatility improved LLY's stock forecast distributions relative to JumpHMM (Table~\ref{tab:stock_validation}). In the original option test, JumpHMM's mean-based stock errors were slightly higher than a Gaussian random walk's (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:forecast_stock}). Reconstructing prices found no growth-to-price conversion error, and initializing the state from past returns had little effect on five-session median errors (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:stock_initialization}). Historical validation selected an EWMA decay of $0.97$ and zero regression drift, making the direction-predicting model identical to the adaptive-volatility model (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:stock_selection}). Neither it nor JumpHMM materially improved on an unchanged price in 2025 or the exploratory 2026 comparison; one-session MAE and RMSE were within 1\% of the benchmark for every method (Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:stock_validation_five} and~\ref{tab:stock_validation_one}). For LLY in 2025, however, adaptive volatility improved on JumpHMM: it lowered five-session CRPS from \$29.60 to \$28.57 and raised central 90\% coverage from 69.9\% to 84.4\%, while widening intervals from \$100.40 to \$151.37. The 2026 LLY comparison changed in the same direction, with CRPS falling from \$35.60 to \$33.81 and coverage rising from 70.6\% to 94.1\%. For GS, adaptive volatility had slightly higher CRPS in 2025 and 20\% higher CRPS in 2026, \$15.58 versus \$13.03. Both models already covered every GS endpoint in 2026, so wider intervals added no coverage. Adaptive volatility tracked LLY's observed daily volatility more closely than JumpHMM's fixed spread but remained too high for GS in 2026 (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:mechanism_checks}). It helped forecast uncertainty for one ticker without establishing a general advantage or improving direction forecasts.

Widening the JumpHMM return cutoff from $b=10$ to $b=20$ left these conclusions unchanged (Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:tail_sensitivity} and~\ref{tab:tail_forecasts}). In the illustrations, mean short-call P\&L at expiration changed by less than \$0.02 per share and its 5\% expected shortfall by less than \$0.07. At the ablation's ten-day endpoint, the corresponding full-factor changes were below \$0.004 and \$0.02. Five-session full-factor option MAE changed by less than \$0.007, and JumpHMM stock CRPS by less than \$0.002. The primary cutoff removed only about 0.017\% of the original emission probability (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:emission_bounds}). Because so few draws approached either bound, this stability did not establish robustness to rare returns beyond them.
