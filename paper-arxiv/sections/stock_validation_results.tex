We next asked whether a different stock forecast could reduce these misses without future information, and found that central forecasts from every tested method remained close to an unchanged-price benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:stock_validation}). Return-scaling checks and reconstruction of the saved paths found no error in the conversion from annualized log growth to daily prices. Conditioning the initial state on recent returns did not materially improve five-session central forecasts, changing median-based stock MAE from \$16.28 to \$16.36 for GS and from \$49.58 to \$49.40 for LLY (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:stock_initialization}), because the effect of the initial state on the forecast distribution largely disappeared within a few sessions under the fitted transitions. We then compared the retained JumpHMM with unchanged-price, adaptive-volatility, and simple directional benchmarks, using earlier data to select the new models' settings. Validation across 2019--2024 selected an EWMA decay of $0.97$ and the zero-drift limit of the directional regression (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:stock_selection}), so the stock and SPY return features did not justify a directional adjustment under this criterion and the selected directional and adaptive forecasts were identical. Testing the frozen models in 2025 supplied 245 five-session origins per ticker, more than fourteen times the 17 available in the 2026 case study, and in both periods central price errors remained close to the unchanged-price benchmark. None of the tested changes repaired the large stock-price misses or gave a consistent central-forecast advantage.

We then scored the full forecast distributions and found a ticker-dependent benefit from adaptive volatility (Table~\ref{tab:stock_validation}). In 2025, LLY's five-session CRPS fell from 29.60 under JumpHMM to 28.57 under the adaptive model, while central 90\% coverage increased from 69.9\% to 84.4\%. Average interval width increased from \$100.40 to \$151.37, so the coverage gain came with a wider range of predicted prices, and the lower CRPS indicated that this tradeoff improved the distribution score even though coverage remained below its nominal level. The 2026 comparison showed the same direction of change for LLY, with CRPS falling from 35.60 to 33.81 and coverage rising from 70.6\% to 94.1\%, and the adaptive model had lower date-level CRPS on 131 of 245 LLY origins in 2025 and ten of seventeen in 2026. For GS, however, CRPS increased slightly in 2025, from 16.22 to 16.32, and by 20\% in 2026, from 13.03 to 15.58, and although both GS models covered every 2026 endpoint, the adaptive intervals were wider. Mean-based RMSE also remained close to the unchanged-price benchmark, and the selected directional forecasts matched the adaptive forecasts exactly (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:stock_validation_five}), while the one-session comparison showed little separation in central price error from an unchanged-price forecast (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:stock_validation_one}). Adaptive volatility therefore improved LLY's forecast uncertainty, not the prediction of stock-price direction, and we retained JumpHMM as the worked example for the IV framework rather than selecting a replacement stock model from these test outcomes.

Finally, we widened the return-emission bound from $b=10$ to $b=20$, repeated the scenario, ablation, forecast, and stock-benchmark simulations, and observed small changes in the reported loss and forecast summaries (Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:tail_sensitivity} and~\ref{tab:tail_forecasts}). Each bound implied state-specific daily return limits (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:emission_bounds}). Widening the bound changed mean terminal short-call P\&L by less than \$0.02 per share and its 5\% expected shortfall by less than \$0.07 in the fitted illustrations, and at the ten-transition coupled-factor ablation endpoint it changed mean call P\&L by less than \$0.004 and expected shortfall by less than \$0.02 (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:tail_sensitivity}). In the forecast comparisons, five-session coupled option MAE changed by less than \$0.007 per share and the reported stock CRPS values by less than \$0.002 (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:tail_forecasts}), so the wider bound did not alter the conclusions of any forecast comparison. These checks compared two specified bounded distributions on the retained simulation ensembles and did not validate either support limit or establish insensitivity to other tail specifications.
