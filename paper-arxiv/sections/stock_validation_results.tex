We next tested whether other stock forecasts could reduce these misses without future information and found that every tested method forecast stock prices about as accurately as an unchanged-price benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:stock_validation}). Before comparing methods, we checked how the JumpHMM forecasts scaled returns and rebuilt prices from the saved paths, and found no error in converting annualized log growth to daily prices. Inferring the starting state from recent returns did not materially improve five-session median forecasts. In these reruns, which used their own seeds, the median-based stock MAE changed from \$16.28 to \$16.36 for GS and from \$49.58 to \$49.40 for LLY (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:stock_initialization}). Under the fitted transitions, the starting state's influence on the forecast distribution largely disappeared within a few sessions. We then compared JumpHMM with the unchanged-price benchmark, the adaptive-volatility model, and the direction-predicting model, using earlier data to select the settings of the last two. Validation on 2019--2024 data selected an EWMA decay of $0.97$ and the zero-drift limit of the direction-predicting model's regression (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:stock_selection}). Under this criterion, the stock and SPY return features added no signal about direction, so the direction-predicting forecasts were identical to the adaptive-volatility forecasts. Testing the models with fixed settings in 2025 supplied 245 five-session origins per ticker, more than fourteen times the 17 in the 2026 case study. In both periods, median-forecast errors stayed close to those of the unchanged-price benchmark. The RMSE of the mean forecast was also close to that of the benchmark (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:stock_validation_five}). One-session forecasts likewise showed little separation in central price error from an unchanged-price forecast (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:stock_validation_one}).

Because these point forecasts did not separate the methods, we then scored the full forecast distributions and found a ticker-dependent benefit from the adaptive-volatility model (Table~\ref{tab:stock_validation}). In 2025, LLY's five-session CRPS fell from 29.60 under JumpHMM to 28.57 under the adaptive-volatility model. Coverage of the central 90\% interval rose from 69.9\% to 84.4\%. Average interval width grew from \$100.40 to \$151.37, so the coverage gain came with a wider range of predicted prices. The lower CRPS showed that this trade-off improved the distribution score, although coverage stayed below its nominal level. In 2026, the LLY forecasts changed in the same direction. Their CRPS fell from 35.60 to 33.81, and their coverage rose from 70.6\% to 94.1\%. The adaptive-volatility model had lower date-level CRPS on 131 of 245 LLY origins in 2025 and on ten of seventeen in 2026 (Table~\ref{tab:stock_validation}). For GS, however, CRPS rose slightly in 2025, from 16.22 to 16.32. In 2026, it rose by 20\%, from 13.03 to 15.58. Both GS models covered every 2026 endpoint, but the adaptive-volatility intervals were wider. The adaptive-volatility model therefore gave LLY forecast ranges that covered more outcomes and scored better, while its median forecasts stayed close to an unchanged price.

Finally, because every JumpHMM simulation used the return cutoff $b$, we widened the cutoff and observed small changes in the loss and forecast summaries (Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:tail_sensitivity} and~\ref{tab:tail_forecasts}). We raised the cutoff from $b=10$ to $b=20$ and repeated the scenario, ablation, forecast, and stock-benchmark simulations. Across all fitted states, each cutoff implied daily return limits for each stock (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:emission_bounds}). In the original GS and LLY illustrations, the wider cutoff changed mean short-call P\&L at expiration by less than \$0.02 per share and its 5\% expected shortfall by less than \$0.07. At the ablation's ten-day endpoint, it changed the full factor's mean call P\&L by less than \$0.004 and its expected shortfall by less than \$0.02 (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:tail_sensitivity}). In the forecast comparisons, five-session full-factor option MAE changed by less than \$0.007 per share. The reported stock CRPS values changed by less than \$0.002 (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:tail_forecasts}). The wider cutoff therefore did not change the conclusion of any forecast comparison.
