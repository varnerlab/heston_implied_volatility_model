We next asked whether a different stock forecast could reduce these misses without future information, and found that every tested method forecast stock prices about as accurately as an unchanged-price benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:stock_validation}). Checks of return scaling and of prices rebuilt from the saved paths found no error in converting annualized log growth to daily prices. Inferring the starting state from recent returns did not materially improve five-session median forecasts. Median-based stock MAE changed from \$16.28 to \$16.36 for GS and from \$49.58 to \$49.40 for LLY (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:stock_initialization}). Under the fitted transitions, the starting state's influence on the forecast distribution largely disappeared within a few sessions. We then compared JumpHMM with the unchanged-price benchmark, the adaptive-volatility model, and the direction-predicting model, using earlier data to select the settings of the last two. Validation on 2019--2024 data selected an EWMA decay of $0.97$ and the zero-drift limit of the direction-predicting model's regression (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:stock_selection}). The stock and SPY return features therefore added no signal about direction under this criterion, so the direction-predicting forecasts were identical to the adaptive-volatility forecasts. Testing the models with fixed settings in 2025 supplied 245 five-session origins per ticker, more than fourteen times the 17 in the 2026 case study. In both periods, median-forecast errors stayed close to those of the unchanged-price benchmark.

We then scored the full forecast distributions and found a ticker-dependent benefit from the adaptive-volatility model (Table~\ref{tab:stock_validation}). In 2025, LLY's five-session CRPS fell from 29.60 under JumpHMM to 28.57 under the adaptive-volatility model, while central 90\% coverage increased from 69.9\% to 84.4\%. Average interval width grew from \$100.40 to \$151.37, so the coverage gain came with a wider range of predicted prices. The lower CRPS showed that this trade improved the distribution score, although coverage stayed below its nominal level. In 2026, LLY changed in the same direction, with CRPS falling from 35.60 to 33.81 and coverage rising from 70.6\% to 94.1\%. The adaptive-volatility model had lower date-level CRPS on 131 of 245 LLY origins in 2025 and on ten of seventeen in 2026 (Table~\ref{tab:stock_validation}). For GS, however, CRPS rose slightly in 2025, from 16.22 to 16.32, and by 20\% in 2026, from 13.03 to 15.58. Both GS models covered every 2026 endpoint, but the adaptive-volatility intervals were wider. Mean-based RMSE also stayed close to the unchanged-price benchmark (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:stock_validation_five}). One-session forecasts likewise showed little separation in central price error from an unchanged-price forecast (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:stock_validation_one}). The adaptive-volatility model therefore improved the range of LLY's forecasts, while its median forecasts stayed close to an unchanged price.

Finally, we widened the emission cutoff from $b=10$ to $b=20$, repeated the scenario, ablation, forecast, and stock-benchmark simulations, and observed small changes in the loss and forecast summaries (Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:tail_sensitivity} and~\ref{tab:tail_forecasts}). Across all fitted states, each cutoff implied daily return limits for each stock (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:emission_bounds}). In the fitted illustrations, the wider cutoff changed mean short-call P\&L at expiration by less than \$0.02 per share and its 5\% expected shortfall by less than \$0.07. At the ablation's ten-day endpoint for the coupled factor, it changed mean call P\&L by less than \$0.004 and expected shortfall by less than \$0.02 (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:tail_sensitivity}). In the forecast comparisons, five-session coupled-factor option MAE changed by less than \$0.007 per share and the reported stock CRPS values by less than \$0.002 (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:tail_forecasts}). The wider cutoff therefore did not change the conclusion of any forecast comparison.
