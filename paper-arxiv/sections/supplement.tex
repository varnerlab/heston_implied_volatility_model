\section{Interpretation of the IV Factor}\label{sec:supp_sigma_decomp}

A continuous-time square-root process motivated the Euler update in~\eqref{eq:euler}. For a fixed target, the process is given by:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:level_sde}
    dv_{a,t}=\kappa(\bar v_{a,t}-v_{a,t})\,dt
    +\sigma_v\sqrt{v_{a,t}}\,dW_{a,t},
\end{equation}
where contract $a$ indexes ticker, strike, and expiration, and the target $\bar v_{a,t}$ from~\eqref{eq:theta} changes with current moneyness and calendar DTE. The stationary mean of this process, when it exists, equals the fixed target $\bar v_a$. The simulation departed from this setting in two ways. The target moved every trading day, and the floor in~\eqref{eq:euler} modified the process. The stationary mean therefore explains why each factor reverts toward its target, but it does not give the simulated distribution.

\FloatBarrier
We calculated Greeks along each path by central finite differences on the same Leisen--Reimer tree that supplied the base price (Algorithm~\ref{alg:greeks}). For GS, delta approached $\pm1$ on paths heading toward exercise, gamma concentrated near the strike, and Vega declined toward zero (Fig.~\ref{fig:gs_greeks}). The figures use the long-option sign convention, so a short position's Greeks have the opposite sign.
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Greeks by central finite differences along each path}\label{alg:greeks}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require State $(S,\sigma,\tau)$ with calendar DTE $\tau$; contract $(K,r)$; LR tree steps $N_{\mathrm{LR}}$; relative spot bump $h_S$; absolute IV bump $h_\sigma$.
\Ensure $\Delta$, $\Gamma$, and Vega.
\State $T_{\mathrm{yr}} \gets \tau/365$.
\State $P_+^S \gets \mathrm{LR}(S(1+h_S),K,\sigma,T_{\mathrm{yr}},r)$ and $P_-^S \gets \mathrm{LR}(S(1-h_S),K,\sigma,T_{\mathrm{yr}},r)$.
\State $P_0 \gets \mathrm{LR}(S,K,\sigma,T_{\mathrm{yr}},r)$.
\State $P_+^\sigma \gets \mathrm{LR}(S,K,\sigma+h_\sigma,T_{\mathrm{yr}},r)$ and $P_-^\sigma \gets \mathrm{LR}(S,K,\sigma-h_\sigma,T_{\mathrm{yr}},r)$.
\State $\Delta \gets (P_+^S-P_-^S)/(2h_SS)$.
\State $\Gamma \gets (P_+^S-2P_0+P_-^S)/(h_SS)^2$.
\State $\mathrm{Vega} \gets 0.01(P_+^\sigma-P_-^\sigma)/(2h_\sigma)$.
\State \Return $(\Delta,\Gamma,\mathrm{Vega})$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
