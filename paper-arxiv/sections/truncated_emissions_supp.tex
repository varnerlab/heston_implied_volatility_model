\section{Return Cutoffs and Sensitivity}\label{sec:truncated_emissions}

Every JumpHMM price simulation used the truncated emissions in~\eqref{eq:truncated_emission}, with $b=10$ as the primary cutoff and $b=20$ as the wider sensitivity case. We set both cutoffs as stress assumptions before rerunning the affected experiments, without reference to the largest observed return. The truncation also applied to the state-inference likelihoods, and each experiment recomputed its pilot or ensemble normalization under each cutoff.

We converted the cutoffs to daily percentage returns using the pilot growth shift from the tests on later dates (Table~\ref{tab:emission_bounds}). These are the widest possible daily returns over all fitted states, not forecast intervals. The removed mass is the probability cut from the standardized Student-$t$ distribution, before state locations and scales are applied. Normalization constants differ slightly between experiments, so their daily limits differ slightly too.

The sensitivity comparison reran the GS and LLY illustrations, the IV ablation, both forecast cohorts, and the stock benchmarks with the same seeds and inputs at each cutoff. We compared short-call loss summaries for the illustrations and for the coupled-factor ablation after ten trading days (Table~\ref{tab:tail_sensitivity}). We also compared five-session coupled option errors in the shorter-maturity cohort and JumpHMM stock scores (Table~\ref{tab:tail_forecasts}). The differences come only from the changed cutoff and the recomputed normalization constants.
