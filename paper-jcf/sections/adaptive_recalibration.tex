\section{Adaptive recalibration via low-rank daily adapters}\label{sec:adaptive}

The pooled multi-date calibration of §\ref{sec:calibration} delivered a single coherent IV surface usable for the forward simulator of §\ref{sec:scenarios}, but it paid for that coherence on two fronts. A per-day Hagan SABR reference fit independently to every (date, ticker, maturity) slice undercut the pooled sector $\psi_{\text{NN}}$ on aggregate RMSE by $1.2\%$ IV ($9.05$ vs $10.24\%$; Table~\ref{tab:three_way}). On individual capture days whose realised IV regime diverged from the multi-date average, the static surface mis-priced both legs of the same-ticker $30\Delta$ pair in the same direction, producing the $-\$1.35/-\$1.80$ entry edges on LLY's 04-28 capture (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:lly_scenario}). Both costs were structural to the multi-date pooling rather than to the architecture itself. We resolved them by introducing a daily low-rank adapter on top of the frozen base $\psi_{\text{NN}}$ (§\ref{sec:lora_method}), gated by a streaming median-and-MAD refit trigger (§\ref{sec:lora_trigger}).

The trigger recovered $74\%$ of the $112$ corpus cells exceeding the $12\%$ RMSE refit-needed threshold at the Youden-optimal operating point ($\tau_z = 0$, $\tau_{\text{abs}} = 10\%$) with $67\%$ precision, and $67\%$ at the more conservative $\tau_z = 1.5$ default with $69\%$ precision (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:lora_trigger_roc}; Fig.~\ref{fig:lora_trigger}). The flagged dates included INTC's 04-21 pre-print, 04-22 print, 04-23 post-print, and 04-28 regime-echo dates; the trigger missed only the 04-14 capture, which fell inside the four-date warm-up window where no streaming statistic could discriminate without trailing data.

\subsection{Empirical sweep across 20 (ticker, date) cells}\label{sec:lora_sweep}

We tested the adapter on 20 cells drawn deterministically from the corpus: the five highest-base-RMSE cells (problem days), the five lowest (controls), and ten cells from the middle 50\% of the RMSE distribution (Table~\ref{tab:lora_sweep}). On each cell we compared two adapter variants against the unadapted base: V0 updated only the scalar $\log\theta_i$ ($1$ parameter), and V1 used the rank-2 LoRA on each Dense layer together with $\Delta\log\theta_i$ (135 parameters). V1 reduced the day-slice RMSE on $20$ out of $20$ cells (it never overfit) and beat V0 on every cell. The improvement increased with base RMSE: median absolute reduction was $20.4\%$ IV on the five worst cells, $2.4\%$ on the middle ten, and $0.7\%$ on the five controls (Fig.~\ref{fig:lora_sweep}A). On the control cells, the adapter barely perturbed the surface: when the base $\psi_{\text{NN}}^{(i)}$ already fit a ticker's day-slice to within $4\%$ IV (the GS cells), the rank-2 adapter shifted the level scalar by less than $0.07$ in $\log\theta$ and the shape perturbation norms $\|B_\ell A_\ell\|_F$ all stayed below $2.7$. On the worst cells, the middle-layer perturbation $\|B_2 A_2\|_F$ increased steadily with the base RMSE (Fig.~\ref{fig:lora_sweep}B), so the adapter put most of its capacity in the middle layer rather than the input or output projection, the same pattern reported for transformer LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora}.

The LLY 04-28 cell appeared as a single row in the sweep. The base $\psi_{\text{NN}}^{(\text{LLY})}$ produced a $15.66\%$ slice RMSE, V0 reduced it to $11.78\%$ by lifting $\log\theta_{\text{LLY}}$ by $+0.35$, and V1 reduced it to $4.34\%$ with a much smaller level shift ($+0.03$) and a shape perturbation that lifted the wings without disturbing the near-the-money region. Re-pricing the $30\Delta$ LLY 2026-05-29 put and call from §\ref{sec:lly_scenario} under each variant produced (Table~\ref{tab:lora_lly_edges}): the base $-\$1.35$ put / $-\$1.80$ call entry edges; V0 overshot to $+\$6.15$ / $+\$5.44$ because the global level lift over-priced the $30\Delta$ strikes while fitting the wings; V1 closed the gaps to $-\$1.15$ / $+\$0.46$, the call leg landing essentially at par. The same comparison on INTC's 04-23 post-print blowup (the largest single failure in the corpus) gave a $68.6\%$ base RMSE, $17.1\%$ under V0, and $5.97\%$ under V1, with $A_\ell, B_\ell$ pattern norms $0.70 / 2.02 / 0.68$ and $\Delta\log\theta_{\text{INTC}} = +0.30$.

\subsection{No-arbitrage diagnostic on adapted surfaces}\label{sec:lora_arb}

The rank-2 LoRA adapter carried no structural constraint to preserve butterfly or calendar arbitrage, so we re-ran the static-arbitrage check of §\ref{sec:supp_static_arb} on the 20 sweep cells under both the base and the V1-adapted $\psi_{\text{NN}}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:lora_arb_audit}). Aggregated across the 20 surfaces, butterfly violations rose from $9.78\%$ to $12.74\%$ of grid points and calendar violations from $1.01\%$ to $2.32\%$. The amplification was bounded but concentrated on aggressive adapters fit to the highest-RMSE cells: INTC 04-23, AMD 04-23, and QCOM 04-23 each saw butterfly violation rates roughly triple under adaptation, while six of the twenty cells (mostly the GS controls) actually saw violations decrease. The natural extension is a Variant 2 that adds an arbitrage-penalty term to the adapter loss --- the LoRA framework imposes no obstacle to such a penalty, and the per-cell adapters could enforce sector-specific weights on butterfly vs calendar consistency --- but we did not implement it here. We report the violation rates as a known property of the rank-2 unconstrained adapter, the cost we pay for the slice-RMSE gain documented above.

\subsection{Operating regime}\label{sec:lora_regime}

The adapter behaved symmetrically across the corpus. Where the multi-date base $\psi_{\text{NN}}^{(i)}$ matched the day's market regime, the rank-2 adapter barely perturbed the surface (sub-percent slice-RMSE improvement, near-zero level shift) and the streaming trigger correctly chose not to refit. Where the day's regime diverged from the calibration-window average---LLY 04-28's elevated-IV regime, INTC 04-22/23's earnings-print and post-print blowup, the residual $+4.36\%$ generalization gap of Configuration~C in Table~\ref{tab:earnings_holdout}---the adapter closed most of the gap with about a hundred trainable parameters per cell, the trigger flagged the right days online, and the forward simulator of §\ref{sec:scenarios} continued to operate on the unadapted base. The adapter is therefore not a daily-refit replacement for the multi-date pooled fit, which would break the forward-simulation property the framework exists to provide; it is a calibration-time overlay that closes the multi-date-vs-daily gap on the subset of days where the base $\psi_{\text{NN}}^{(i)}$ alone is insufficient.
