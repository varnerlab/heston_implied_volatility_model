\section{Adaptive recalibration via low-rank daily adapters}\label{sec:adaptive}

The pooled multi-date calibration of §\ref{sec:calibration} delivered a single coherent IV surface usable for the forward simulator of §\ref{sec:scenarios}, but it paid for that coherence on two fronts. A per-day Hagan SABR reference fit independently to every (date, ticker, maturity) slice undercut the pooled sector $\psi_{\text{NN}}$ on aggregate RMSE by $1.2\%$ IV ($9.05$ vs $10.24\%$; Table~\ref{tab:three_way}). On individual capture days whose realised IV regime diverged from the multi-date average, the static surface mis-priced both legs of the same-ticker $30\Delta$ pair in the same direction, producing the $-\$1.35/-\$1.80$ entry edges on LLY's 04-28 capture (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:lly_scenario}). Both costs were structural to the multi-date pooling rather than to the architecture itself. We resolved them by introducing a daily low-rank adapter on top of the frozen base $\psi_{\text{NN}}$.

\subsection{Adapter formulation}\label{sec:lora_method}

Each Dense layer of the base $\psi_{\text{NN}}^{(i)}$ for ticker $i$ contained a weight matrix $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$. We froze $W$ and added a rank-$r$ perturbation $\Delta W = \tfrac{\alpha}{r}\, B A$ with $A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d_{\text{in}}}$, $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times r}$, and a scalar scaling $\alpha$~\cite{hu2022lora}. Initialisation followed the original prescription: $A$ drew from a small Gaussian and $B$ was set to zero, so the adapter started as a no-op and only departed from the base in directions favoured by the daily-slice loss. We trained one adapter per (ticker, capture-date) cell with rank $r = 2$ and $\alpha = 2$ across the three Dense layers, jointly with a scalar update $\Delta\log\theta_i$ on the per-ticker level; the architecture mirrored the base $\psi_{\text{NN}}^{(i)}$ exactly so the adapted forward pass was
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lora_forward}
    \sigma^2_{\text{model}}(K, T, t) = e^{\,\log\theta_i + \Delta\log\theta_i} \;\cdot\; \psi_{\text{NN}}^{(i),\,\text{LoRA}}\!\bigl(\widetilde{\ln T},\,\widetilde{\ln(K/S_t)};\, W + \tfrac{\alpha}{r} B A\bigr).
\end{equation}
For the operating per-ticker architecture ($2 \to 16 \to 16 \to 1$ Dense with $\tanh$ activations), the rank-$2$ adapter carried 135 trainable parameters per cell: $\{A_\ell, B_\ell\}_{\ell=1,2,3}$ with sizes $(r{\times}d_{\text{in}}^{(\ell)}) + (d_{\text{out}}^{(\ell)}{\times}r)$, plus the one-scalar level update. By contrast, the base $\psi_{\text{NN}}^{(i)}$ carried $369$ parameters and the per-day SABR reference of §\ref{sec:calibration} fit three parameters per maturity bucket. The adapter trained for up to $1{,}500$ epochs of Adam against the same MSE-against-market-IV loss in~\eqref{eq:nn_loss}, restricted to the day's ladder slice (typically $200$--$1{,}000$ rows), with a step-decay learning-rate schedule and patience-150 early stopping; convergence was typically reached by epoch 300--600. The forward-simulation pipeline of §\ref{sec:scenarios} was unaffected by the adapter: the base $\psi_{\text{NN}}^{(i)}$ remained the calibration target for the multi-date ladder corpus, and the adapter served only as a mark-to-market overlay applied at the day of contract entry. Forward variance paths under Algorithm~\ref{alg:forward_sim} continued to use the unadapted base $\bar\theta_i$ and $\psi_{\text{NN}}^{(i)}$ exactly as before.

\subsection{Streaming refit trigger}\label{sec:lora_trigger}

A daily refit on every (ticker, date) cell would defeat the framework's compute argument and reintroduce the SABR-style independent-slice failure mode. We therefore used a streaming trigger that decided online whether to fit an adapter for ticker $i$ on date $t$. The trigger fired when both an absolute and a relative condition held: today's base-vs-market RMSE on the ticker's slice exceeded an absolute floor $\tau_{\text{abs}}$ ($10\%$ IV in our experiments), and its robust z-score over the trailing four \emph{non-flagged} dates exceeded a relative threshold $\tau_z$. The robust z-score was
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trigger_z}
    z_t \;=\; \frac{\text{RMSE}_t \;-\; \mathrm{median}(\mathcal{T}_t)}{1.4826 \cdot \mathrm{MAD}(\mathcal{T}_t)},
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{T}_t$ the most recent four dates on which the trigger did \emph{not} fire. The flagged-day exclusion was load-bearing: in pilot runs that used a plain mean--standard-deviation baseline, the post-INTC-print 04-23 blowup contaminated the trailing window and hid the more moderate 04-28 LLY-style regime mismatch (which our LoRA prototype subsequently showed required adaptation). The robust median--MAD with exclusion correctly flagged INTC's 04-21 pre-print, 04-22 print, 04-23 post-print, and 04-28 regime-echo dates in the corpus, missing only the 04-14 capture which fell inside the trigger's four-date warm-up window. Across the full $31 \times 15$ corpus ($457$ filtered cells), $112$ cells exceeded the $12\%$ RMSE refit-needed threshold; the trigger at the Youden-optimal operating point ($\tau_z = 0$, $\tau_{\text{abs}} = 10\%$) recovered $74\%$ of those with a precision of $67\%$, and at the more conservative $\tau_z = 1.5$ default recovered $67\%$ with a precision of $69\%$ (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:lora_trigger_roc}; Fig.~\ref{fig:lora_trigger}). The remaining false negatives concentrated on the warm-up window, where no streaming statistic could discriminate without trailing data.

\subsection{Empirical sweep across 20 (ticker, date) cells}\label{sec:lora_sweep}

We stress-tested the adapter across a sweep of 20 cells drawn deterministically from the corpus: the five highest-base-RMSE cells (problem days), the five lowest (controls), and ten cells stratified through the middle 50\% of the RMSE distribution (Table~\ref{tab:lora_sweep}). On each cell we compared two adapter variants against the unadapted base: V0 updated only the scalar $\log\theta_i$ ($1$ parameter), and V1 used the rank-2 LoRA on each Dense layer together with $\Delta\log\theta_i$ (135 parameters). V1 reduced the day-slice RMSE on $20$ out of $20$ cells (it never overfit) and dominated V0 on every cell. The improvement scaled cleanly with base RMSE: median absolute reduction was $20.4\%$ IV on the five worst cells, $2.4\%$ on the middle ten, and $0.7\%$ on the five controls (Fig.~\ref{fig:lora_sweep}A). The control-day behaviour was the right kind of near-no-op: when the base $\psi_{\text{NN}}^{(i)}$ already fit a ticker's day-slice to within $4\%$ IV (the GS cells), the rank-2 adapter shifted the level scalar by less than $0.07$ in $\log\theta$ and the shape perturbation norms $\|B_\ell A_\ell\|_F$ all stayed below $2.7$. On the worst cells, the middle-layer perturbation $\|B_2 A_2\|_F$ tracked the base RMSE almost monotonically (Fig.~\ref{fig:lora_sweep}B), confirming that the adapter mass concentrated in the intermediate representation rather than the input or output projection, the same pattern reported for transformer LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora}.

The case that motivated the prototype reappeared as a single row in the sweep. On LLY's 04-28 capture, the base $\psi_{\text{NN}}^{(\text{LLY})}$ produced a $15.66\%$ slice RMSE, V0 reduced it to $11.78\%$ by lifting $\log\theta_{\text{LLY}}$ by $+0.35$, and V1 reduced it to $4.34\%$ with a much smaller level shift ($+0.03$) and a shape perturbation that lifted the wings without disturbing the near-the-money region. Re-pricing the $30\Delta$ LLY 2026-05-29 put and call from §\ref{sec:lly_scenario} under each variant produced (Table~\ref{tab:lora_lly_edges}): the base $-\$1.35$ put / $-\$1.80$ call entry edges; V0 overshot to $+\$6.15$ / $+\$5.44$ because the global level lift over-priced the $30\Delta$ strikes while fitting the wings; V1 closed the gaps to $-\$1.15$ / $+\$0.46$, the call leg landing essentially at par. The same head-to-head reproduced on INTC's 04-23 post-print blowup (the largest single failure in the corpus): the base produced a $68.6\%$ slice RMSE, V0 reduced it to $17.1\%$, and V1 reached $5.97\%$ with $A_\ell, B_\ell$ pattern norms $0.70 / 2.02 / 0.68$ and $\Delta\log\theta_{\text{INTC}} = +0.30$.

\subsection{No-arbitrage diagnostic on adapted surfaces}\label{sec:lora_arb}

The rank-2 LoRA adapter carried no structural constraint to preserve butterfly or calendar arbitrage, so we re-ran the static-arbitrage check of §\ref{sec:supp_static_arb} on the 20 sweep cells under both the base and the V1-adapted $\psi_{\text{NN}}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:lora_arb_audit}). Aggregated across the 20 surfaces, butterfly violations rose from $9.78\%$ to $12.74\%$ of grid points and calendar violations from $1.01\%$ to $2.32\%$. The amplification was bounded but concentrated on aggressive adapters fit to the highest-RMSE cells: INTC 04-23, AMD 04-23, and QCOM 04-23 each saw butterfly violation rates roughly triple under adaptation, while six of the twenty cells (mostly the GS controls) actually saw violations decrease. The natural extension is a Variant 2 that adds an arbitrage-penalty term to the adapter loss --- the LoRA framework imposes no obstacle to such a penalty, and the per-cell adapters could enforce sector-specific weights on butterfly vs calendar consistency --- but we did not implement it here. We report the violation rates as a known property of the rank-2 unconstrained adapter, the cost we pay for the slice-RMSE gain documented above.

\subsection{Operating regime}\label{sec:lora_regime}

The empirical picture is symmetric to the framework's calibration story. Where the multi-date base $\psi_{\text{NN}}^{(i)}$ matched the day's market regime, the rank-2 adapter perturbed the surface negligibly (sub-percent slice-RMSE improvement, near-zero level shift), and the streaming trigger correctly chose not to refit. Where the day's regime diverged from the calibration-window average---LLY 04-28's elevated-IV regime, INTC 04-22/23's earnings-print and post-print blowup, the residual $+4.36\%$ generalization gap of Configuration~C in Table~\ref{tab:earnings_holdout}---the adapter closed most of the gap with $\mathcal{O}(100)$ trainable parameters per cell, the trigger flagged the right days online, and the forward simulator of §\ref{sec:scenarios} continued to operate on the unadapted base. The adapter is therefore best read not as a daily-refit replacement for the multi-date pooled fit (which would surrender the forward-simulation property the framework exists to provide), but as a calibration-time overlay that closes the multi-date-vs-daily gap on the subset of days where the base $\psi_{\text{NN}}^{(i)}$ alone would otherwise be insufficient.
